WordPinyinExplanation
叔叔阿姨
HSK3		shū shu ā yíUncle and Auntie — the standard respectful address for adult friends' parents or neighbors, regardless of actual relation. Children in China use this for any adult couple. Here the boy uses it charmingly to defuse the awkward situation with the parents.
早就
HSK4		zǎo jiù"Long ago; have… for a long time already." 早就知道了 = knew long ago. 早 (early) + 就 (already/then) = even earlier than expected. The phrase implies the speaker is ahead of events. Used to show one was not deceived. Compare: 已经 (already, neutral timing).
看出来
HSK4		kàn chū láiTo see through; to figure out from observation. Directional complement 出来 = "out from observation." 我早就看出来了 = I saw through it long ago. Compare: 看出 (see/detect, without the 来 that implies the realization "surfacing").
怎么可能
colloquial		zěn me kě néng"How could it possibly be?" — a rhetorical exclamation of disbelief or denial. 怎么 (how) + 可能 (possible). Very common in spoken Chinese. Compare: 不可能 (impossible, a flat denial); 怎么可能 is more incredulous and conversational.
不然
HSK4		bù rán"Otherwise; if not." 不然她该不给我讲题了 = otherwise she won't explain problems to me anymore. Functions as a connective adverb. Near-synonym: 否则 (otherwise, more formal/written). Very common in spoken Chinese for conditional warnings.
我就说嘛
colloquial		wǒ jiù shuō ma"I told you so; didn't I say so?" — a smug retrospective claim. 就 here means "precisely/exactly." 嘛 is a particle asserting something is obvious. The father uses this to cover up that he actually did NOT know, then immediately gets called out by the mother asking "since when?"
