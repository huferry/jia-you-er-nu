WordPinyinExplanation
绝对得
colloquial		jué duì děi绝对 (absolutely) + 得 (děi = must). Note: 得 here is the modal verb meaning "must/have to" (děi), NOT the structural particle 得 (de) or the verb 得 (dé = obtain). This tone distinction is important: 得 as "must" is always děi in spoken Mandarin.
严厉
HSK5		yán lìStern, strict, severe. 严厉的惩罚 = severe punishment. Used of discipline, criticism, or consequences. Stronger than 严格 (strict/rigorous, more about rules/standards). 严厉 often implies harshness with an emotional component; 严格 is more neutral/procedural.
猖狂
HSK5+		chāng kuángRampant, brazen, wildly arrogant. Usually describes enemies, criminals, or misbehaving people acting without restraint. Here Xiaoyu uses it hyperbolically about his sister bringing a boyfriend home — a child's over-the-top moral outrage. Near-synonym: 嚣张 (arrogant/cocky).
挨打
HSK4+		ái dǎTo get beaten/hit; to take a beating. 挨 (ái) = to suffer/endure (always tone 2 when meaning "receive/suffer"). 挨打 is a set collocation. Note 挨 ≠ āi (approach/near). Other 挨 collocations: 挨骂 (get scolded), 挨饿 (suffer hunger). Very colloquial in family/school contexts.
犯法
HSK4		fàn fǎTo break the law; to be illegal. 犯 = to commit (an offense); 法 = law. 犯法 emphasizes the act of violation. Near-synonyms: 违法 (violate the law, more formal/official); 违规 (violate rules, not necessarily criminal). The scene satirizes Chinese parents who threaten to hit kids.
打110
colloquial		dǎ yāo yāo língTo call 110 — China's police/emergency number (equivalent to 911 in the US). 打 here means "dial/call" (打电话 = make a phone call). The humor: calling the police on your own parents is ironic since after the police leave, you're alone with the same parents again.
