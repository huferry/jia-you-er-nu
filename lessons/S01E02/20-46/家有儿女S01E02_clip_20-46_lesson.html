WordPinyinExplanation
所作所为
idiom		suǒ zuò suǒ wéiOne's actions and conduct — everything one does. A formal 4-character phrase (chéngyǔ-style) used to judge someone's overall behavior. Slightly literary; in spoken Chinese it often signals serious reproach: 你们的所作所为让我很失望 = "I am very disappointed by everything you've done."
家长
HSK3		jiā zhǎngParent, guardian; head of a family. Note: 长 is zhǎng (elder/leader), NOT cháng (long). 跟家长说 = "tell the parents/adults." Collocations: 家长会 (parent-teacher meeting), 家长制 (patriarchal system). Distinct from 父母 (parents specifically).
说一声
phrase		shuō yī shēng"Say something / give a heads-up." 跟家长说一声 = "let the parents know / just tell someone." 一声 (one sound/utterance) makes it casual and minimal — just a brief word, not a full report. Very common in family/social contexts.
向…学
grammar		xiàng … xuéTo learn from someone. 向你学 = "learned from you." 向 (towards/from) + person + 学 (learn). Common pattern: 向老师学, 向前辈学 (learn from teachers/seniors). The children use it here cheekily to deflect blame onto the adults.
才…呢
grammar		cái … neEmphatic counter-accusation: "YOU'RE the one who…!" 你才过分呢 = "YOU'RE the one who's out of line!" The pattern 你才…呢 pushes the charge back at the accuser. Stress is on 才 (it's precisely YOU, not me). Very common in bickering among children and siblings.
过分
HSK4		guò fènExcessive, going too far. Used twice in rapid succession here (lines 10 and 11) — once as accusation, once as rebuttal. The word becomes a verbal ping-pong ball in the argument. 太过分了 (too much) vs 你才过分 (you're the one who's out of line).
