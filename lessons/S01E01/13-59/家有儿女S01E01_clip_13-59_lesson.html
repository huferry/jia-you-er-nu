WordPinyinExplanation
敲门
HSK4+		qiāo ménTo knock on a door. 敲 = to knock/tap. A key social courtesy in Chinese homes — Xiaoxue is insisting on this as a formal rule, which is unusual in a traditional Chinese family context where parents often enter children's rooms freely. The comedy is in codifying normal Western-style privacy norms as a binding legal declaration.
保持沉默
HSK4+		bǎo chí chén mòTo maintain silence / to remain silent. 保持 = maintain (HSK4); 沉默 = silence, taciturn (HSK4). The phrase echoes Miranda rights ("you have the right to remain silent") — deliberate humor. 沉默是金 = silence is golden (proverb). Compare: 安静 (quiet — describes the environment); 沉默 (silence — describes a person's choice not to speak).
请勿打扰
formal		qǐng wù dǎ rǎoPlease do not disturb. 请 = please; 勿 = do not (classical/formal negative, more emphatic than 不要); 打扰 = to disturb, to bother (HSK4). The same phrase is on hotel door signs. Its use in a family dinner speech is comically formal. 打扰一下 = excuse me (polite way to interrupt someone).
有权
HSK4+		yǒu quánTo have the right to (do something). Pattern: 有权 + V. 我有权不回答 = I have the right not to answer. Compare: 权利 (quánlì, right/entitlement as a noun); 权力 (quánlì, power/authority). 有权 is the verb phrase used in legal and rights discourse — Xiaoxue is parodying a legal rights declaration throughout her speech.
西红柿
HSK3+		xī hóng shìTomato. Northern Chinese term (common in Mandarin). Southern Chinese often say 番茄 (fān qié). 西红柿鸡蛋汤 = tomato and egg soup — a quintessential Chinese home-cooking dish. Its appearance here is emotionally significant: it was Xiaoxue's favorite as a child, and 刘美 (the stepmother) made it to welcome her, an act of kindness Xiaoxue cannot quite ignore.
从小
HSK3		cóng xiǎoSince childhood, from a young age. Fixed adverbial phrase. 从小就 + V = has [done X] since childhood. Compare: 小时候 (when [I] was little — a time noun used as a topic or adverbial); 从小 is more naturally adverbial. 你从小就特别爱吃这个 = you've loved this ever since you were little.
