WordPinyinExplanation
不像话
HSK4+		bù xiàng huàOutrageous, unacceptable, out of line. Literally "not like speech/words" → doesn't behave as one should. 太不像话了 = that's completely unacceptable! A common spoken exclamation expressing moral disapproval. Used by adults about children's behavior, or by anyone about conduct that violates social norms. Compare: 不像样 (doesn't look the part / shabby); 说不过去 (can't justify it).
去你的
colloquial		qù nǐ deGet out of here! / Oh come on! / You wish! A dismissive exclamation used to brush off an unreasonable suggestion or to tease someone. Not rude, more like rolling one's eyes. 刘美 says it when her husband suggests going to scold Xiaoxue immediately — she's dismissing his impulsive idea. Compare: 得了吧 (give me a break); 才不是呢 (no way).
主见
HSK5		zhǔ jiànOne's own opinion / ability to make independent decisions. 没主见 = spineless, indecisive, can't think for oneself. 有主见 = knows one's own mind, decisive. 刘美 uses it as a pointed jab: if the dad scolds Xiaoxue because 刘美 suggested it, he's just a puppet — 没主见的爹. This is both an accusation and a warning: she doesn't want to be manipulated into that role.
后妈
colloquial		hòu māStepmother. 后 = behind/after (in family terms: step-); 妈 = mom. 毒后妈 = wicked/cruel stepmother — a stock villain from fairy tales (like Cinderella's stepmother). 刘美 is acutely aware of this stereotype and is strategically managing her image with Xiaoxue. The 毒 (venomous, cruel) is what she wants to avoid being labeled as.
忍
HSK4		rěnTo endure, bear, put up with, restrain oneself. 只好忍着 = can only put up with it / have no choice but to endure. 忍 is a core concept in Chinese social culture — patience and endurance are virtues. Compounds: 忍耐 (patience, endurance); 忍不住 (can't hold back, can't help but); 忍气吞声 (swallow one's anger — idiom for bearing humiliation in silence).
只好
HSK4		zhǐ hǎoHave no choice but to, can only. 只好 + V = the only option is to [do X]. Expresses that all other options are closed off. 咱们俩只好忍着 = the two of us can only grin and bear it. Compare: 只能 (can only — focuses on ability/possibility); 不得不 (have to, must — stronger compulsion); 只好 emphasizes reluctant acceptance of the only remaining option.
